# δ-opioid agonism and seizure liability — evidence for the Registry part pages

**Prepared for the iGEM Registry documentation of the Epione basic and composite parts.**
Every reference below was retrieved from Europe PMC or ClinicalTrials.gov and the quoted
claim read from the primary abstract. PMIDs and DOIs are given so a judge can check each one.

---

## Summary in one paragraph

δ-opioid agonist-induced seizures are **real, reproducible and δ-receptor-mediated** — but the
evidence is overwhelmingly from **high-efficacy, non-peptidic, systemically administered small
molecules** (BW373U86, SNC80). The authoritative 2023 review states plainly that agonists
*"range in their propensity to induce seizure behavior, with multiple δOR agonists reportedly
not causing seizures"*, so this is a **ligand-specific property, not a class effect**. For
**peptide** enkephalins the evidence points the opposite way: the δ-selective enkephalin
analogue DADLE is anticonvulsant through δ receptors, and raising endogenous Met-enkephalin by
blocking its degradation **reduced** seizures. Most directly, a vector expressing **human
preproenkephalin — the same payload class as ours — completed Phase 1 in cancer-pain patients
with no agent-related serious adverse events**, and went on to a completed Phase 2.

---

## A · The liability is real, and it is δ-receptor-mediated

Do not let anyone leave the poster thinking we dismissed this.

**1.** The nonpeptidic δ-selective agonist **BW373U86 produced a brief, non-lethal convulsion
in mice**. Critically, the δ-selective antagonist **naltrindole** shifted its convulsant
potency rightward in a dose-dependent manner — establishing the effect as δ-mediated rather
than off-target.
*Comer SD, Hoenicke EM, Sable AI, et al. J Pharmacol Exp Ther. 1993;267(2):888-95.*
PMID 8246164 · doi:10.1016/s0022-3565(25)39544-3

**2.** In rats, nonpeptidic δ agonists produced **bilateral ictal and paroxysmal spike and
sharp-wave EEG discharges** at doses previously reported to give antidepressant-like effects —
electrographic seizure activity, not merely overt convulsion.
*Jutkiewicz EM, Baladi MG, Folk JE, Rice KC, Woods JH. J Pharmacol Exp Ther. 2006;317(3):1337-48.*
PMID 16537798 · doi:10.1124/jpet.105.095810

**3.** Mechanism is now partly localised: **δ receptors on parvalbumin-expressing interneurons
are necessary** for SNC80's convulsant effect. Conditional deletion diminished convulsant and
anxiolytic effects while sparing locomotor stimulation, so the seizure mechanism is
anatomically separable from other δ effects.
*Behav Pharmacol. 2026.* PMID 42213967 · doi:10.1097/fbp.0000000000000882

**4.** Efficacy is a determinant. Comparing the partial agonist BU48 with the full agonist
SNC80 shows δ-mediated antihyperalgesia, antidepressant-like effects and convulsions have
**different efficacy requirements and receptor reserve**.
*J Pharmacol Exp Ther. 2020;374(1):167-176.* PMID 32467352 · doi:10.1124/jpet.119.262717

## B · But it is ligand-specific, not a property of δ agonism as such

**5.** The comprehensive review of this exact question concludes that δOR agonists *"range in
their propensity to induce seizure behavior, with multiple δOR agonists reportedly not causing
seizures"*, and that there is *"a significant gap in our current understanding of why certain
δOR agonists are more likely to induce seizures"*.
*Neuropharmacology. 2023;231:109526.* PMID 37004753 · doi:10.1016/j.neuropharm.2023.109526

**This is the single most important citation on the page.** It is the difference between
"δ agonists cause seizures" (false as a general claim) and "some δ agonists cause seizures,
and we should establish which class ours belongs to" (true, and answerable).

---

## C · For peptide enkephalins the evidence points the other way

**6.** The δ-selective enkephalin analogue **DADLE has an anticonvulsant action** in rats, and
low doses of the δ antagonist ICI 154,129 **selectively antagonised that anticonvulsant
action** — so the protective effect is itself δ-receptor-mediated. (Higher antagonist doses
raised seizure threshold on their own.)
*Eur J Pharmacol. 1984;98(1):149-51.* PMID 6321207 · doi:10.1016/0014-2999(84)90523-5

**7.** Raising endogenous enkephalin **reduced** seizures. Intracisternal protease inhibitors —
including **phosphoramidon, a neprilysin inhibitor** — decreased both seizure incidence and
seizure score dose-dependently in seizure-susceptible El mice. **Naloxone reversed it**, so the
effect ran through opioid receptors, and the authors attributed it to increased endogenous
Met-enkephalin.
*Neurochem Res. 1993;18(11):1183-7.* PMID 8272192 · doi:10.1007/bf00975045

> **This reference is directly on point for our second arm.** Our siRNA knocks down
> neprilysin (MME) to extend payload half-life. Pharmacologically that is the same
> intervention as phosphoramidon in this study — and the reported outcome was **fewer**
> seizures, not more.

**8.** Review arguing that acute opioid receptor activation has **anticonvulsant effects**,
modulating microglial activation as a route to suppress seizures in temporal lobe epilepsy.
*Front Neurol. 2023;14:1298489.* PMID 38249734 · doi:10.3389/fneur.2023.1298489

## D · The direct clinical precedent — the same payload, in patients

**9.** **NP2** — a replication-defective HSV vector expressing **human preproenkephalin** —
completed a multicentre, dose-escalation **Phase 1** trial in patients with intractable cancer
pain. The primary outcome was safety, and the result was: *"Treatment was well tolerated with
no study agent-related serious adverse events observed at any point in the study."* Subjects in
the middle- and high-dose cohorts reported pain relief.
*Fink DJ, Wechuck J, Mata M, Glorioso JC, Goss J, Krisky D, Wolfe D. Ann Neurol. 2011;70(2):207-12.*
PMID 21796661 · doi:10.1002/ana.22446

**10.** The field explicitly describes this payload as a δ agonist — HSV vectors *"engineered
to produce inhibitory neurotransmitters, including the delta opioid agonist peptide
enkephalin"* — and traces the path from preclinical work to that human trial.
*Wolfe D, Mata M, Fink DJ. Gene Ther. 2009;16(4):455-60.* PMID 19242524 · doi:10.1038/gt.2009.17

**11.** ClinicalTrials.gov **NCT00804076** — "Gene Transfer for Cancer Pain", intervention NP2,
Phase 1, status **COMPLETED**.

**12.** ClinicalTrials.gov **NCT01291901** — "NP2 Enkephalin For Treatment of Intractable
Cancer Pain", NP2 vs placebo, Phase 2, status **COMPLETED**.

## What we can and cannot claim from this

**Can claim.** δ-agonist seizure liability is established for high-efficacy non-peptidic small
molecules and is ligand-specific rather than a class effect; peptide enkephalins and
enkephalinase inhibition are reported as anticonvulsant through opioid receptors; and a human
preproenkephalin gene-transfer product completed Phase 1 in the same indication with no
agent-related serious adverse events.

**Cannot claim, and should say so on the page.**

1. **NP2 expressed native preproenkephalin. Ours is engineered** — seven `YGGFEVVG` cassettes
   in place of the native Met/Leu-enkephalin cores. The precedent covers the payload *class*,
   not our exact sequence.
2. **Route differs.** NP2 was delivered peripherally to dorsal root ganglia; our intended route
   is intranasal, which carries a different CNS-exposure profile. This is the gap most worth
   flagging to a judge, because it is the one a pharmacologist will raise.
3. **References 6 and 7 are from 1984 and 1993**, in seizure-susceptible rodent models. They
   are directionally important but are not modern safety pharmacology.
4. **No seizure study has been done on our peptide.** Nothing here substitutes for that.

**One point that cuts in our favour, from our own data.** Our calibrated selectivity estimate
puts `YGGFEVVG` at **+1.23 ± 1.76 kcal/mol** — nominally δ-preferring but statistically
indistinguishable from no preference, and indistinguishable from native Met-enkephalin. The
convulsant literature concerns *high-efficacy, strongly δ-selective* agonists. Our failure to
demonstrate strong δ-selectivity, which we report honestly as a negative result elsewhere,
**reduces exposure to this specific liability**. We should not pretend that was the plan — but
it is the accurate reading of where the risk sits today.
